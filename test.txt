MixFormerV2 is a fully Transformer, one-stream architecture for visual object tracking that jointly processes target-template, search-region, and prediction tokens without dense convolutional heads. The authors argue that it improves on prior Transformer trackers because it replaces the computationally expensive dense corner-regression head and elaborate score-prediction module with lightweight token-based MLP heads, thereby producing a simpler, more unified model with a substantially better accuracy–latency trade-off on both GPUs and CPUs. Technically, four learnable prediction tokens—corresponding to the top, left, bottom, and right bounding-box coordinates—are concatenated with the template and search tokens and propagated through prediction-token-involved mixed-attention modules, where they progressively aggregate target–search correlations; a shared MLP then predicts a probability distribution for each coordinate, from which the final location is obtained by expectation, while a two-layer MLP estimates target confidence from the mean of the four tokens. Efficiency and accuracy are further preserved through dense-to-sparse distillation, which converts the teacher’s two-dimensional corner distributions into one-dimensional coordinate marginals and transfers them using KL divergence, and deep-to-shallow distillation, which combines feature imitation and logit matching with progressive layer elimination, an intermediate teacher for very shallow models, and reduction of Transformer-MLP dimensions. Empirically, MixFormerV2-B reaches 70.6% AUC on LaSOT, 57.4% on TNL2K, 83.4% on TrackingNet, 69.9% on UAV123, and 0.556 EAO on VOT2022 while operating at 165 GPU FPS; the compact MixFormerV2-S achieves 60.6% LaSOT AUC at 325 GPU FPS and 30 CPU FPS, outperforming FEAR-L by 2.7 AUC points and demonstrating competitive real-time tracking under limited computation.
